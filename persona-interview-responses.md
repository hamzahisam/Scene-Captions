# Persona interview responses

## User A: woman, 60s–70s, Urdu-first, very conservative, late hearing loss

### A. Viewing habits and language preferences

1. **Do you usually watch videos with captions or words on the screen?**

   **Answer** On the big TV at home, no. The cable channels don't give any words, so I turn the volume up and watch the faces. On my phone I sometimes switch on the English words for a missed episode, but I read them slowly and the scene runs away from me.

2. **When you watch videos with family, who decides whether captions are used and how they look?**

   **Answer** On the lounge TV my son and daughter-in-law decide, and they don't like anything written over the picture. On my own tablet I decide, and nobody touches my settings.

3. **Which language do you prefer for captions: Urdu, English, or either?**

   **Answer** Urdu, without question. When I read \[دروازہ زور سے بند ہوا\] (the door slammed shut) I understand at once; the English I have to put together word by word.

4. **Which language do you prefer for the app's buttons, menus and instructions: Urdu, English, or either?**

   **Answer** Urdu. I managed the buttons, but some were still in English, like "Preview" and "Profile", and that made me stop and think.

5. **Are any caption words difficult to understand? Which version is easier: simple wording or more detailed wording? (Show examples rather than assuming reading ability from age.)**

   **Answer** The short ones were easy. I understood the long one, \[اوپر کی منزل پر بھاری دروازہ زور سے بند ہوا اور آواز گونج اٹھی\] (a heavy door slammed upstairs and the sound echoed), but by the time I finished it the scene had changed. So simple words, with a little more only where it matters.

### B. Sound-caption preferences

6. **Which sound description do you prefer: "\[door bangs\]," "\[BANG\]," or "\[loud sharp sound\]"? (Show the same clip with each version.)**

   **Answer** "Door bangs", in Urdu "دروازہ زور سے بند ہوا". "دھڑام" is what children's comics write, and "loud sharp sound" tells me nothing; it could be a glass breaking or a slap.

7. **When you cannot see what made a sound, would you prefer a short description or more information? (Show both versions.)**

   **Answer** A little more, when I can't see it. "In the other room" told me someone had walked out angry before the camera showed it. Plain "door bangs" made me look around the picture for a door that wasn't there.

8. **When you can already see an action, such as a door closing, do you still want a caption describing its sound?**

   **Answer** No. If I can see the son shutting the door, I don't need it written. Only if he slams it so hard that it matters to the story.

9. **For music, which description do you prefer: "\[slow piano music\]" or "\[sad music\]"? (Use examples that accurately describe the clip.)**

   **Answer** "Slow piano music", in Urdu "پیانو کی دھیمی دھن". I still remember those soft piano tunes from the old dramas, and reading it brings the tune back. "Sad music" I can see for myself on the mother's face.

10. **Would you prefer captions for every sound or only selected sounds? Which sounds matter most to you?**

    **Answer** Only the ones that matter: the doorbell, the phone, a knock, someone crying in another room, the azaan. I don't need every spoon in the kitchen written down.

11. **Which detail level do you prefer: Essential, Balanced or Rich? (Show actual examples of all three.)**

    **Answer** Balanced (متوازن). The short one sometimes left me guessing where the sound came from, and the detailed one was too much reading for my eyes.

12. **Which sounds were easy to understand from the captions, and which were difficult?**

    **Answer** Easy: the doorbell and the door slam. Difficult: the fighting part of the English film. The words changed so fast I only caught "explosion" and lost the rest.

### C. Reading effort and consistency

13. **When captions appear quickly, is it difficult to watch what is happening in the picture?**

    **Answer** Yes, in the fighting scene very much; I was reading and missed who fell down. In the kitchen scene it was fine.

14. **How much effort did reading the captions take? (Adult rating: 1 = Very little; 7 = Very much.)**

    **Answer** 5/7. The Urdu letters were small at first, and even after I made them bigger I was leaning towards the tablet with my glasses pushed up.

15. **How comfortable were the captions to read? (Adult rating: 1 = Very uncomfortable; 7 = Very comfortable.)**

    **Answer** 3/7. Once I made the Urdu bigger I could read it, but then the black strip sat over the son's chin and mouth, and I like to watch mouths.

16. **Did your eyes feel tired, or did reading become tiring as the video continued? When did you notice this?**

    **Answer** Yes, after about ten minutes, around the fighting part, my eyes started watering and I took my glasses off for a moment. At home I'd have to stop after one episode like this.

17. **How consistent were the descriptions when the same sound returned? Would you prefer the same wording or different wording? (Adult rating: 1 = Very inconsistent; 7 = Very consistent. Show examples and invite an explanation.)**

    **Answer** 3/7. The doorbell said a long thing the first time and just "گھنٹی" (bell) the second time, so I thought it was a different bell. When the same thing happens, write the same words.

### D. Caption layout and app controls

18. **Are sound captions easier to follow in their own area or together with dialogue captions? (Show both layouts.)**

    **Answer** Together at the bottom, in one place. When the sound words were in a separate corner at the top, I kept missing them, because my eyes are always at the bottom.

19. **Where do you prefer captions: beside the sound source, at the top, or at the bottom? Does the position cover anything important? (Show matching examples; some placements may be comparison concepts rather than implemented options.)**

    **Answer** At the bottom, the way it has always been on TV. The words beside the door looked clever, but my eyes would be running all over the screen. And at the bottom, when the letters are big, it covers faces; that needs fixing.

20. **How much control did you feel you had over the captions? (Adult rating: 1 = Very little; 7 = Very much.)**

    **Answer** 4/7. I could change things once I found them, but the app also changed the words by itself in the middle, and I didn't ask for that.

21. **Could you keep your preferred settings or turn automatic changes off when you wanted?**

    **Answer** The young man showed me that my choice stays, which is good. The switch to stop it changing by itself I would never have found alone; it was hidden under something called "Advanced".

22. **Were the caption settings easy to find and use? Was anything confusing? (Also observe the participant finding and changing a setting.)**

    **Answer** The little wheel in the top corner was too small and pale; I touched the screen two or three times before the menu opened. The three choices were clear, but "منظر کے مطابق تبدیلی" (change according to scene) I didn't understand; change what? \[Observer: Taps the video twice, pausing it, before finding the gear icon. Changes level to متوازن in about 50 s. Increases font size only after being reminded the size control exists. Tilts her head to read through the lower part of her bifocals.\]

23. **Did the examples and previews help you understand what would change? (Observe the participant trying a preview.)**

    **Answer** The little sample box helped me see which words would change, yes. But its letters were smaller than in the video, so I couldn't judge the size from it. \[Observer: Opens the preview and compares the short and detailed samples aloud, reading slowly. Leans in to the preview pane and chooses Balanced. Has to readjust font size after returning to the video because the preview size differed.\]

24. **Would you like the app to remember your caption and language choices next time?**

    **Answer** Yes, I don't want to set it up every time. But my grandchildren use this tablet too; it should stay mine and not change if they press things.

### E. Review of the revised app

25. **After trying the updated app, were the problems you mentioned earlier addressed? Which changes worked for you, and which did not?**

    **Answer** (projected) First I'd check the Urdu letter size, and whether the strip still sits on the faces. If I can read from my sofa with my glasses on and still see the mouth, that's fixed. Then the doorbell, same words both times; if the app still changes words by itself without telling me, I'd say nothing was fixed.

26. **What else would you change before using the app to watch a full film?**

    **Answer** (projected) A bigger settings button, everything in Urdu, and an easy pause. For a full film the words must stay calm in the fighting parts, or I'll be tired before the interval.

- **Where everyone agrees:** name the source of the sound, and use identical wording when a sound repeats. The scene-aware feature broke this in front of every persona. Hold the wording of a recurring sound fixed across detail levels, and either default scene-aware to off or announce each change. Move the off switch out of Advanced.
- **Music wording splits by hearing history.** A, B and E prefer descriptive wording (memory, or because "sad" is obvious from faces). C needs interpretive wording, and D wants mood or nothing. Make descriptive vs interpretive a separate choice rather than tying it to detail level.
- **Urdu is where the effort lands.** The Urdu-reading elders show the highest effort and lowest comfort. This comes from Nastaliq size and how much picture the box covers, not from the wording. They need an Urdu default size and line height of their own, a narrower box kept clear of mouths, a preview that renders at the real size, and an interface that is fully translated (including "Preview", "Profile" and a clearer label for "Show Original"). A Naskh option is worth testing.
- **Layout and naming should be choices.** A, B and D want sound and dialogue together at the bottom; C and E want a separate area. C and E also want profiles that follow them to the phone or TV, while A and D want profiles that never change unasked. Plain level names ("Short / Medium / Detailed") would help B and E.
